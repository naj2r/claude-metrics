## TWO HEADLINE FINDINGS

### Headline #1 — The substantive coefficient (single-vote analysis)

After conditioning on French-language share + Catholic share, vineyard area per capita predicts higher yes-vote share on the 1908 Swiss absinthe ban (vote #68). Subset-denominator KEY spec: **β = +484.4, p = 0.024**. Total-pop denominators (matches prior Brainstorm-Absinthe analysis to 4+ decimals): **β = +437, p = 0.063**.

The bivariate coefficient is negative (β = −178.6, p = 0.34) — a Simpson's-paradox sign-flip. Conditioning on language flips it to positive. Robust across LOO (all 25 cantons, sign stable), exclude-NE+GE, RI 10k perms (p = 0.031), fractional logit AMEs, all 5 weighting schemes, all 6 alternative vineyard operationalizations, German-only subsample, continuous-language weighting, pre-determined 1894 vineyard, and all new-control specs.

Substantive translation (`t11_magnitudes.tex`): one-SD vineyard increase → +4.0 pp higher yes-vote; Vaud vs Uri → +11.2 pp shift.

### Headline #2 — Stiglerian regulatory capture (multi-vote panel)

Cross-referendum falsification on all 15 federal popular votes 1900-1910 reveals the absinthe ban is the **second of two wine-industry rent-seeking victories**:
- Vote #65 (1906 Lebensmittelgesetz / federal food-purity law): vineyard β = **+1286, p = 0.045** — adulteration crackdown disadvantaged wine competitors. NOT a clean placebo: it's the regulatory infrastructure later invoked against absinthe.
- Vote #68 (1908 absinthe ban): β = +484, p = 0.024
- Vote #63 (1903 federal alcohol-trade regulation, no clear wine-industry benefit): β = **−52, p = 0.886** (clean null) — vineyard cantons did NOT systematically oppose federal alcohol regulation
- All 11 other 1900-1910 federal votes: vineyard coefficient is null

Three-vote sequence (null on #63, positive on #65, positive on #68) is the canonical empirical signature of **Stigler (1971) regulatory capture**: industries support regulation when and only when it disadvantages competitors. Quantitative triangulation of a process Prestwich (1988), Padosch et al. (2006), and Studer (2024) document qualitatively.

**Full discussion**: `analysis/documentation/progress/progress_2026-04-30_1830_foodbev.md` (~500-line in-depth note covering background, data, translation, Stigler integration, paper restructuring sketch).

### Mechanism-level finding (Gelbach decomposition)

Of the −663 sign-flip (b1base = −178.6 → b1full = +484.4):
- **delta_LANG (french_share contribution): −573.9, p = 0.044** ← 86.6% of the flip
- delta_RELIG (catholic_share contribution): −89.1, p = 0.336 ← 13.4% of the flip

The Simpson confound is overwhelmingly a language story; religion is essentially irrelevant. Auxiliary regression: vineyard_per_cap explains 26.4% of variance in french_share (ρ=0.51) — strong but not perfect overlap. Top wine cantons split 4 French / 5 German / 1 Italian. Schaffhausen (German) is the #1 wine canton per capita.

3. **Gelbach (2016) decomposition** to attribute Simpson sign-flip to language vs religion. Implemented via canonical `b1x2` package (vendored, v4.1.0) with hand-validated identity check. Methods reference saved with sources.

4. **DATA-RECENCY RULE for canton-level controls** (codified in CONTEXT.md): controls must use 1908-or-earlier data, OR data within a few years post-vote where time-stability is defensible. The 43-year gap to 1951 fruit-tree data does NOT meet this bar; `fruit_tree_density` was tested briefly then EXCLUDED from active reporting (variable retained for provenance).

5. **`avg_parcel_area_1905`** is constructed from `agland_1912 / (farms_1905 × parcels_per_farm_1905)` because I.39c block 4 (mittlere Parzellenfläche) has no 1905 row. Cross-validated against block 4 1929 values. Worst data gap is 4 years (agland_1912).

7. **Both `french_share` and `german_share` used** ("two sides of the coin"). Subset and total-pop denominators for both, parallel to existing variants. Not exact complements (Italian/Romansh).

8. **Skill: `/major-change`** + `methods-doc-reminder.sh` hook. Major substantive findings get documented as `progress_YYYY-MM-DD_HHMM_<topic>.md` permanent notes. Methods references in `documentation/methods/` auto-surface in future sessions on keyword match (post-compact safe).
